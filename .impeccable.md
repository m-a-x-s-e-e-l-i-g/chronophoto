## Design Context

### Users

Action photographers and creators shooting parkour, skateboarding, BMX, dance,
climbing, and athletics. They arrive with a short video clip, want to select the
decisive movement, and need a finished motion composite without hand-masking a
dozen frames in Photoshop.

### Brand Personality

Exact, restrained, and utilitarian. Chronophoto should feel like a specialist
imaging terminal: confident and precise, never playful for its own sake.

### Aesthetic Direction

A monochrome workspace inspired by contact sheets, frame counters, and terminal
interfaces. Near-black surfaces, white controls, gray metadata, and fixed-width
type keep attention on the photograph. Dense enough for repeated professional
use, but built around one obvious flow: load, frame, compose, export. Avoid
generic dashboard cards, colorful accents, neon styling, and effects that
compete with the photograph.

### Design Principles

1. The photograph is always the largest and brightest object in the interface.
2. Make the happy path possible in three decisions: range, poses, export.
3. Reveal technical mask controls progressively and label them in plain language.
4. Use motion only to explain processing and state changes.
5. Keep every operation local and communicate that privacy advantage clearly.
